\begin{NoteBox}[Das Leben in einer lebendigen Stadt]
Das Leben in einer lebendigen Stadt bringt jeden Tag neue Eindrücke und
unerwartete Momente mit sich. Während morgens die Sonnenstrahlen langsam durch
die \colorbox{SoftGold}{\strut Nebelschleier} brechen und die Straßen im
\colorbox{GermanGold!30}{\strut goldorangenen} Licht erstrahlen, erwacht das
städtische Treiben zum Leben.

\medskip
\underline{Dennoch} schätzen \textcolor{GermanRed}{viele} Menschen die ruhigen
Zufluchtsorte wie Parks oder schattige Cafés. Dort kann man bei einer heißen
Tasse Kaffee entspannen, dem Alltagsstress für einen kurzen
\textbf{Augenblick} entfliehen und neue Energie sammeln.

\medskip
\textit{Reflection:} Today I noticed that the conjugated German verb normally
occupies the second position. I should practise changing the subject while
keeping the rest of the sentence stable.

\vspace{2\baselineskip}
\end{NoteBox}

\LessonSection{Writing Notes}

Write four sentences that introduce your name, home city, occupation, and
reason for learning German.

\begin{InformationBox}[Writing pattern]
Use the pattern \GermanText{Ich lerne Deutsch, weil~\ldots} to explain a reason.
Remember that the conjugated verb moves to the end of a \emph{weil} clause.
\end{InformationBox}

\begin{WarningBox}[Writing warning]
Do not write a comma between the subject and verb. Use a comma before a
subordinate clause introduced by \emph{weil}.
\end{WarningBox}

\begin{QuoteBox}[Writing model]
Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich später in Deutschland arbeiten möchte.
\end{QuoteBox} %
\LessonSection{Reading Notes}

In short introductions, German speakers often state their name with
\GermanText{Ich heiße~\ldots} or \GermanText{Mein Name ist~\ldots}.

\begin{InformationBox}[Reading strategy]
Read once for the general meaning, then underline names, verbs, and unfamiliar
words. Use the surrounding sentence before checking a translation.
\end{InformationBox}

\begin{WarningBox}[Reading warning]
Do not translate every German word in isolation. Compound nouns and context can
change the meaning you expect from individual word parts.
\end{WarningBox}

\begin{QuoteBox}[Reading text]
Jeden Morgen liest Lea einen kurzen deutschen Artikel und notiert drei neue
Wörter in ihrem Lernbuch.
\end{QuoteBox} %
\LessonSection{Speaking Notes}

Say the dialogue aloud twice. Then repeat it without reading and replace the
names and personal details with your own.

\begin{InformationBox}[Speaking strategy]
Practise complete phrases rather than isolated words. Record yourself, listen
once for pronunciation, and repeat the sentence at a natural speed.
\end{InformationBox}

\begin{WarningBox}[Speaking warning]
Do not sacrifice clarity for speed. Pay particular attention to German vowel
length and the final consonants in words such as \emph{Tag} and \emph{Hund}.
\end{WarningBox}

\begin{QuoteBox}[Speaking prompt]
Hallo! Ich heiße Alex. Ich komme aus London und lerne seit drei Monaten Deutsch.
\end{QuoteBox} %
\LessonSection{Listening Notes}

Listen for the reduced spoken form \GermanText{Wie geht's?} and note whether
speakers use the informal \GermanText{du} or formal \GermanText{Sie}.

\begin{InformationBox}[Listening strategy]
Listen first without pausing. On the second pass, write down keywords. On the
third pass, check endings, numbers, names, and any words you initially missed.
\end{InformationBox}

\begin{WarningBox}[Listening warning]
Spoken German often joins words together. Do not assume that every sound you
cannot separate represents a new or unknown word.
\end{WarningBox}

\begin{QuoteBox}[Listening transcript]
Guten Morgen! Heute sprechen wir über das Leben in einer deutschen Stadt.
\end{QuoteBox} %

\LessonSection{Grammar Rules}
\grammarentry{Present tense!sein}
\begin{GrammarBox}[The present tense of \textit{sein}]
The verb \GermanText{sein} (to be) is irregular. Its form changes with the subject.
German statements normally place the conjugated verb in the second position:
\GermanText{Ich bin heute müde.}
\end{GrammarBox}

\subsection*{Examples}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \GermanText{Ich bin Studentin.} --- \translation{I am a student.}
  \item \GermanText{Er ist heute in Berlin.} --- \translation{He is in Berlin today.}
  \item \GermanText{Wir sind Freunde.} --- \translation{We are friends.}
\end{itemize} %
